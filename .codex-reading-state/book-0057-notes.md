## Argument synthesis

### Branzi’s two alternatives

- Andrea Branzi’s 1985 “We Are the Primitives” says modernism’s ideological parachute no longer unifies design/world culture.
- Without shared ideology, identity/moral purpose appears to come from:
  1. personal codes, weak/flexible identities, pluralism and potentially tribal factions;
  2. a replacement general ideology, exemplified by Branzi’s later “second modernity.”
- Buchanan rejects both:
  - personal codes without evaluative standards can turn diversity into power struggle;
  - second modernity turns Eurocentric modernity into cultural/commercial export and repeats power/control.
- He preserves the need for Branzi’s aesthetic experimentation while refusing aesthetic self-expression as sufficient moral account.

### Culture as activity, not ideology

- Buchanan defines culture as cultivation: continuous ordering, disordering and reordering in search of understanding and values.
- Culture is not one fixed doctrine or a set of isolated ideologies; it is a framework in which plural cultures interact and communicate.
- This move supports radical pluralism but seeks shared deliberative grounds rather than relativism.

### Essential contestability and four ends

- Contemporary technological resources shift deliberation from scarce means toward desired circumstances, purposes and consequences.
- Design’s ends are essentially contested; Buchanan proposes four coordinated terms:
  - good;
  - useful;
  - just;
  - pleasurable.
- Pleasure is not opposed to responsibility; producing pleasure is one responsibility, limited and integrated by the other three.
- The four are heuristic commonplaces, not fixed universal meanings; their meaning must be argued in projects.

### Orders of design

- Earlier framework: communication (signs/words/images), construction (physical things), action (activities/services/processes), strategic/systemic thought (systems, environments, ideas, values).
- Designers move from images and objects toward earlier participation in product development and strategic planning.
- `Third-order design`: action-oriented design/strategic planning for activities, services and processes.
- `Fourth-order design`: systemic integration, organizational learning, knowledge-based/networked organizations and plural knowledge.
- Earlier communication/construction disciplines do not disappear; they are re-situated within action and integration.
- Matrices are heuristic mappings, not empirical classifications of every design practice.

### Human abilities and disciplines

- Invention, judgment, decision and evaluation are treated as general human abilities.
- Disciplines enable these abilities; talent alone is insufficient.
- Design history should therefore include the formation of design thinking and disciplines through new problems, not only products, styles or ideas.

### Systems and management critique

- Early systems thinking/systems engineering is characterized as materialist, reductive and means-oriented.
- Quality control’s radical potential is collective human agency: workers/managers discuss quality and modify processes.
- Total Quality Management may remain top-down, materialistic and quantitative.
- Process reengineering often produces downsizing without redistributing worker creativity; IT can function as control.
- Strategic planning breaks down during cultural transformation because plans require but cannot create community vision.
- Fourth-order systemic integration aims at dialogue, organizational learning and multiple forms of knowledge.
- These claims are theoretical/period action judgments; management outcomes need organizational and labor evidence.

### Third alternative: mediated deliberation

- Buchanan opposes binary modern/postmodern columns and proposes a three-term dialectic:
  - opposing claims;
  - a mediated middle of uncertainty, hypothesis, possibility and cooperation.
- Collaborative projects suspend total ideological agreement to reach practical, revisable agreements on present action.
- Theory remains accountable to objective consequences of what is made.
- Design is proposed as a concrete cultural practice capable of bringing affected people into deliberation.

### Dewey/McKeon humanism

- Drawing on Dewey, mind is part of unfinished nature and intentional operations can direct outcomes.
- Drawing on Richard McKeon, culture is a framework within which multiple cultures interact.
- Individuality is relational, not erased by group work; identity and moral purpose arise from finding one’s place among others and examining good/just/useful/pleasurable consequences.

### Designing for the individual

- Buchanan rejects universal audience, nation, market segment and abstract “consumer” as final design subjects.
- The proposed subject is an individual situated in immediate context, supported to participate actively in culture.
- Products become “personal pathways” through cultural ecology.
- This remains normative and individualist; it needs disability, collective rights, class, race, gender, colonialism, public institutions and structural inequality.

## Critical boundaries

- “Culture wars” and twentieth-century tribalism examples are vague; do not equate marginalized groups’ demands with tribal power without evidence.
- The paper acknowledges exclusion by elite design but quickly calls the view simplistic; needs histories by excluded/affected groups.
- Inclusion rhetoric does not specify recruitment, representation, compensation, consent, veto, appeals or decision rights; it is not equivalent to participatory design or industrial democracy.
- “Technological means no longer scarce” is overgeneralized and ignores unequal access, maintenance, resources, colonial extraction and infrastructure.
- “Objective and demonstrable worth” is not operationalized.
- Quality movement description risks celebrating collaboration while understating hierarchy and labor conflict.
- Individual-context focus can understate collective identities, public goods, structural constraints and political organization.
- “Good/just/useful/pleasurable” requires situated contestation and cannot be scored as a universal checklist.
- Branzi’s Eurocentric export proposal is critically rejected, but Buchanan’s own conceptual canon remains largely US/European male philosophy/design/management.
- Race, gender/sexuality, disability, colonialism, environment and supply chains are thin.
- 1990s organizational and technology claims cannot support current platform/AI conclusions.

## Textbook use

- Design theory/professionalization: four orders, design disciplines and strategic/systemic expansion.
- Design ethics: four contested ends and deliberation.
- Design education: disciplines enabling invention/judgment/decision/evaluation; not a school history.
- Organization/design management: critique of TQM, reengineering and strategic planning.
- Participatory design: only as a contrast—consultative inclusion is not decision participation.
- Postmodern/Italian radical design: secondary actor debate, not Branzi intellectual-history final source.
